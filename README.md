# OIR Working Group

Working group chartered out of the HAVI grant application, *Encoding Ambiguity: Embracing Uncertainty in a Medieval Knowledge Graph*. Its job is the community and standards work the proposal describes but cannot itself deliver.

## Why this group exists

The grant funds a medieval Irish knowledge graph. Funding does not settle the questions a durable scholarly resource has to answer anyway: who it is for, what it covers, and how it represents what it knows. Those decisions get made regardless. The only question is whether they get made deliberately, in the open, with the people who will live with them in the room.

## What the charter has to define

The charter is the group's first deliverable. It must cover at minimum:

- **Users.** Who the resource is for, and in what order of priority when those users disagree.
- **Data scope.** What is in, what is out, and who decides.
- **Encoding.** The standards used to represent the data, and how ambiguity is carried rather than erased.
- **Attribution.** How contributors, sources, and institutions are credited.
- **Assertion.** How a claim enters the record, and what confidence it carries once it is there.

## Current state

The group is forming now. A draft charter (see `charter.md`) and a short recommendation memo (see `FINAN_RECOMMENDATION.md`) have been prepared to illustrate how the working group can operate without requiring immediate funding.

## Recommendation Summary

- **Labor structure:** Technical lead (Patrick Cuba) and infrastructure lead (Bryan Haberberger) own the AI/LLM and platform work; Finan focuses on leadership, scholarship, and community engagement.
- **First‑year deliverable:** A concrete worked example of a contested medieval entity encoded with ambiguity‑preserving RDF★STAR and provenance.
- **Governance:** A provisional charter (v0.1) defining Users, Data Scope, Encoding, Attribution, and Assertion, with all sections marked `UNVERIFIED` where evidence is still pending.
- **Next steps:** Convene a virtual kickoff (draft agenda in the charter), invite repository owners (DRI, VRTI, Logainm, others), and schedule a month‑long survey of existing data sources.
